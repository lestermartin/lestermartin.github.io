ATL50 - Leg 5; Medlock Bridge to Island Ford (+ repeat of leg 2 = 15 miles!)

2013-08-17 · Lester Martin

Our intended destination for today's trip was Island Ford at the Chattahoochee River NRA HQ.  This is the third canoe trip this summer that involved Island Ford.  Gretchen and I tested the new canoe rack out as part the First Canoe Ride of 2013 and then she got out on the river again for ATL50 - Leg 2; Island Ford to Azalea Park which was a blast.  I should have known it was going to be good luck for Tony and I today and it actually wasn't our stoping point for the day, but more on that later.

Despite the forecast for rain, Tony and I went for it.  On this trip, Gretchen helped us out by dropping us off (and ultimately picking us up) which took away the usual car shuffle we have to go through due to only having a single vehicle setup to carry the iScanoe (see ATL50 - Leg 4; Abbotts Bridge to Medlock Bridge stickers!).  We left Medlock Bridge around 7:45am for what was going to be our longest trip yet; 11 miles.  The intended plan was to take a break six miles in at Holcomb Bridge and then push on through the last fives miles to Island Ford.  As usual, we were quickly greeted with some ducks.

As well as some geese.

They were just before we went under the rusted remains of (only half!) of the old Jones Bridge.

Another half mile down the river we encountered the Jones Bridge Shoals.  The water was high so we quickly tore through this section.  Maybe Tony and I are getting better at navigating through these rougher sections, or maybe when I keep both legs in the boat it all just works out that much better.  We were laughing through these shoals which I've decided is the best way to attack them.  That, and to accept we'll probably flip the darn boat sooner or later!

Next up was Holcomb Bridge and right after we went under the modern day bridge, we paddled up next to this old stone piling that once supported an earlier incarnation of Holcomb Bridge.  

I'm so grateful these old hunks (like the other abandoned bridges I've been blogging about) are present.  They give the river some character and make you think of the past when these bridges were very active.

With all the overcast clouds, the cool upper 60s temps and the faster than normal current, we made great time on the first six miles.  Tony started getting motivated and was already asking me if we should go on past Island Ford.  We both got a bit fired up, but agreed to get there first before making any big decisions.

These next five miles were a treat.  The picture below shows what we saw 98+% of the time.  

The remoteness you feel is very surreal as you know the Northern Atlanta Metro is all around this river, but the trees (not to mention the big blocks of federally protected lands) make you think you are so far away from civilization and allows you relax like few other activities can.  I can admit it – I LOVE CANOEING THE 'HOOCH!!

When we finally arrived at Island Ford (11 miles knocked out in 3.5 hours) we called Gretchen and found out her car was still at the shop (i.e. the reason we got the drop off/pick up treatment) so we decided to repeat ATL50 - Leg 2; Island Ford to Azalea Park.  Of course, it was only a repeat for me as Tony didn't do it the first time.  We had a super fun time through the shoals that start just after the launch and had a smooth trip the rest of the way to Azalea Park.  That took us to just under 15 miles and a running total (including the two out of boat breaks) of 4.5 hours.

This trip got us back on track for completion and well past the half-way mark with 32 out of 50 miles behind us (not counting the leg 2 repeat) as you can see on ATL50 - Canoeing thru the Chattahoochee River NRA.  More importantly for those visually-oriented folks, Kayaking & Canoeing Google Map now shows a continuous line from Buford Dam down to Azalea Park.  We'll wrap the whole adventure up in three or four more day trips and we'll have earned our "patch"!
