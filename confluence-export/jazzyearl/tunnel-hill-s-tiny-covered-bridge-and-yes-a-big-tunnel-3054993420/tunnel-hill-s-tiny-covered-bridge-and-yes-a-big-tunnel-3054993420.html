Tunnel Hill's tiny Covered Bridge and... Yes, a BIG Tunnel

2021-10-10 · Lester Martin

Driving back and forth from Atlanta, GA, and Chattanooga, TN, I’ve seen the “See a Civil War Tunnel” billboard along the interstate a number of times, but I wasn’t sure what to expect in the little town of Tunnel Hill, GA. Well, a TUNNEL of course, but I let the secret out of the bag that this train tunnel was not as impressive as the Stumphouse Tunnel I captured images of in my Waterfalls In & Around Greenville, South Carolina blog posting.

The Bridge

As a fan of Covered Bridges, I was happy to find a little, and cute, one here in Tunnel Hill that might might be shorter than Georgia's Shortest Covered Bridge. It is called the Clisby Austin Covered Bridge and as you can see, it is more of a decorative bridge than a true wooden structure at its base.

Despite it’s tiny size, and its steel beams supported by concrete, I really liked this cute little thing.

The Museum

The tunnel itself is what the whole town is named for. Lots of good information is available at the official website; https://www.civilwarrailroadtunnel.com/. The 90 minute guided tour was only $12 and you can be lazy (like I tend to be!) and ride on an eight passenger golf cart for the whole experience. Tickets are purchased at the museum itself.

The small museum was worth exploring before the tour started. You find out that the tunnel was guarded by a rebel 25 soldier artillery battery towards the end of the Civil War.

The soldiers abandoned their post when they caught word of General Sherman and the 150,000 man army bearing down on their location on their push to Atlanta.

I surely don’t have the bandwidth to explain the Civil War here or even just that final push that ended in the burning of Atlanta, but I’ll talk “generals” again in a bit as at this point, it was time to head down to the tunnel. I did get some good advice before leaving the museum.

As this tunnel was exceptionally tight for trains, there we are few “safety pockets” that a person could press themselves into in case they were caught in the tunnel when a train was approaching and could not get out in time.

Once you find the end of the tunnel there is just a big turnaround (since this track hasn’t been used since the 1920’s) and you get a great example of how invasive kudzu is in Georgia.

Then back into the tunnel for the reverse journey along this 1477 foot long journey.

The House

The last stop on the tour was the Clisby Austin House which was constructed in 1848 alongside the railroad tunnel.

During the Civil War, the was was used by the North and the South as an army field hospital.

General John Bell Hood, namesake of Fort Hood, recuperated here for a while after losing his right leg which they say was buried here on the grounds SOMEWHERE!

General William T. Sherman, whom the M4 Sherman tank was named after, spent a week here planning his march to Atlanta.

Looking at the fields around the house, it is hard to imagine the bloody combat that took place here.

For those living in Atlanta, I do recommend you give the Tunnel Hill Heritage Center & Museum a visit on one of those trips that you are head up I-75 towards Chattanooga, TN. Don’t forget to tip you tour guide!!
