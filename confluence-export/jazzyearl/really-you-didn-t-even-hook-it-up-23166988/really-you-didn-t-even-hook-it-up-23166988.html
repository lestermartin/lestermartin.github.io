Really... You Didn't Even Hook It Up?!?!

2014-04-20 · Lester Martin

I think anyone reading this posting knows how I excited I was when We Closed on the NEW House!! and got to move in.  I'm still loving the house, but if there is one thing that I absolutely hate it is a "lack of attention to detail."  Maybe it was those hard-ass drill sergeants from Basic Training (that was in the 80's; ah... the 80's, but I digress) who beat it into me, but if you're going to do something then do it right, not half-ass.  What is this thing that has got me so darn fired-up?  It is the sprinkler system on /wiki/spaces/ATM/pages/16187394.

When doing the walk-through inspections of the house I conceded to skip an inspection of the sprinkler system for some reason; weather I think was what was said.  That was mistake #1.  If I know one thing, it is to double-check others work – even if it frustrates them.  I'm not about micro-managing, or even trying to tell folks they did something wrong, but I do believe completely in the "trust, but verify" model.  I really have to blame myself as I trusted before I verified on this one.  The anger started back on the 14th.  I got home on time that day and grabbed my son to help me check out the sprinkers.

I simply couldn't get them to run a manual cycle and was getting frustrated.  I finally took off the lower cover of the Hunter X-CORE control unit and found the first problem.

Yep!  The zones were NOT even connected.  This really got me boiling inside.  It meant that the installers felt so confident in how they installed everything that they didn't even need to actually connect the various zones to the control unit.  Heck, that's MY PROBLEM/TASK it seems.  My blood still boils when I think about this.

A few turns of the screwdriver got all five zones connected, but I had to rearrange them once I saw where each went to create a "wrap around the house" pattern with the zones.  I got Zone 1 setup to work near the control unit in the back yard.  Inside the fence there was a small dry spot in front of the HVAC units, but the bigger problems are outside the fence.  The one below shoots nicely out onto the back alley, but doesn't really get the water in the tiny strip it is there for.

I also have a small corner that has no sprinkler head to get it wet.

I noticed my neighbor's system seems to have a head there...

The coverage for the rest of Zone 1 is pretty good, but I'm going to have to see if I can adjust the heads so they don't constantly spray on the fence as I remember from my Texas home how bad that eats the wood up. 

Zone 2 simply does not spray up into the bushes area (with a few exceptions) as seen in the next few pics.  The neighbors sprinkers in my back yard (I'm trying to not focus on the fact that they layout of the yards compared with the sprinker systems is a bit off, so I'll just march forward without saying another word about that confusion) seem to get the bushes in front of them with no problems.

  

The "misses" continue to wrap around the rest of the house.  Including the side and front of the porch on the side of the house near the back yard.

No surprise it rolls back down the other side (in front of the porch, on the side of it, and down the full length of the house by the garage).

  

  

  

Maybe it isn't as critical as in North Texas, but I'm still of the believe that the soil around the foundation of the house needs to get some water on it. 

The biggest disappointment was Zone 5 which tries to water the back corner of my home. As you can see from the "before" and "after" videos, these sprinkler heads simply will not come up without manually pulling them up.  They reflect a piss-poor installation and worse, a lack of quality control.  VERY, VERY, VERY, trying...

Even once they are operational, they simply don't "hit" the corner near the garage or the corner near the driveway from the alley.

  

All in all, I'm highly disappointed and truthfully embarrassed to publish the posting, but I just as much needed to vent as to have a documented list of concerns to share with the warranty team.  I hope this helps them understand the problems and to get this system to a functioning and accurate state.
