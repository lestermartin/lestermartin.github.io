The Power of an Email (Thanks Despair.com)

2012-06-17 · Lester Martin

I’ve been thinking about starting this blog for a long time. As you see in the “about tics” page it is called "tics" which stands for Tourette (Syndrome) Is Connor’s Story and while focused on my son, it is intended to give others with Tourette Syndrome (TS) a chance to hear about similar folks in their shoes and to raise awareness & understanding of TS to those not as directly impacted. I finally decided to act on this desire to start this blog thanks to the overwhelming positive response I received when I raised a concern to a company I felt was ridiculing TS.

I ran across a t-shirt for sale on www.despair.com that said “Just pretend I have Tourette’s”. I immediately took offense to it and felt compelled to send them an email with the following message because of this apparent ridiculing of TS.

I just wanted to say I have been a big fan of the Despair posters for years.  For Father’s Day I mentioned to my wife to buy me a calendar and was horrified when she pointed out your “Just Pretend I Have Tourette’s” T-Shirts.  My son has Tourette Syndrome (TS) and I made it a practice to boycott any product/show/comedian/etc that makes fun of this disease.  Personally, until I saw this shirt and the one about ADHD, I never was bothered by anything from Despair.  Your company has chosen to make fun of the things we all face (as well as our own false self-importance). 

I also make it a practice to point out to anyone who inadvertently makes fun of Tourette Syndrome to point them to some actual information.  It is far less “funny” than our society makes it out to be.  Anywho, I’d appreciate it you can forward the PDF available at http://www.tsa-usa.org/aPeople/LivingWithTS/Images/Fact_Sheet.pdf to the folks in your very creative product development department.  I know they think it is funny, but watching my son, who is now in 7th grade, deal with this his whole life I can tell you it isn’t funny.  It is also not funny for him, and all the folks dealing with TS to have to see t-shirts like this and to hear jokes in the entertainment industry.

I’m pragmatic enough to know nothing will come of this, but I do request you company reconsider producing & selling shirts like this one.  To do my part as an unhappy consumer, I will be sure to notify all of my friends and family (personally, via email & phone, and on my social media accounts on Facebook and LinkedIn) of my dissatisfaction with your choice to make fun of this disease and to ask them to boycott Despair as well.

I sent that message at 10pm on a Friday night. Image my surprise when I was checking my email on Saturday morning and receiving the following reply from Despair’s CEO.

Thank you for taking the time to write us and share this with us.  I'm very sorry that this particular shirt is hurtful to you and your family.  It's very easy for us in the joke t-shirt business to sometimes write something in a bubble of our own jadedness and/or ignorance.  But we generally try not to create things that might be seen as cruel or hurtful.  

The Tourette's shirt is something we've received a lot of feedback about- including from a customer who has Tourette's and bought the shirt as a way of trying to help people around him have greater comfort with the awkwardness that sometimes arises from his condition.  He gets laughs by being self-deprecating which has been an icebreaker for him.  

Before he wrote us, we didn't really think about the way the shirt would be perceived by those with Tourette's.  We just knew people who we thought the shirt would be good for- and didn't really know anyone who has been directly affected by Tourette's.  But because we'd received an endorsement from a person with Tourette's who had a self-mocking sense of humor, we probably deluded ourselves into thinking that meant the shirt couldn't be offensive to the others affected by Tourette's.   

That is why your email is so appreciated.  Because its convicting to me to read your mail and picture the struggles of a 7th grade boy having to live with the burden of Tourette's.  7th grade is hard enough as it is. And I can't imagine how much harder it might be for your son and for your family.  And in seeing things from your perspective, I can't help but agree. And I don't want Despair to be selling something that would add to the difficulties your son has to deal with.  

I will let the team know we'd like to pull the shirt from the site.  It'll take a few days to get though the process, but by this time next week, you shouldn't see it on the Despair site any longer.

Thanks for taking to time to share this with us.  If it had been a different sort of mail, a rant or something of that sort, it might not have crossed my radar, and we'd have been the poorer for it.

Best regards,

Justin Sewell, CEO Despair, Inc.

I really was blown away and quickly emailed Mr. Sewell back notifying him of my appreciation on behalf of all people affected by TS. True to his word, the www.despair.com website is free & clear of this terrible t-shirt and I couldn’t be more thankful to him and Despair for being so responsible and thoughtful.

So, be sure to visit Despair on Facebook, www.facebook.com/DespairInc, and register an enthusiastic “Like” for their very professional response. They surely do have some extremely funny stuff on their site. I can’t wait to buy a few full color lithographs, www.despair.com/lithographs.html, to adorn the office walls!
