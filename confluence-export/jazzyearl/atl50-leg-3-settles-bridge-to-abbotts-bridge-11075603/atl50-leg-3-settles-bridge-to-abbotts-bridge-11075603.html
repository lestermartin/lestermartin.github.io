ATL50 - Leg 3; Settles Bridge to Abbotts Bridge

2013-07-14 · Lester Martin

It has been raining for a couple of weeks off and on here in Atlanta which has really put a damper on the canoeing.  Let me be clear, I'm sure not complaining about this much rain in summer as I remember the vast majority of my life where I lived in DFW and ANY rain during the summer is a blessing.  In fact, my grass is getting very tall and as soon as I finish this log entry up I'd better go cut it!  Again, NOT complaining!!  In fact, keep the rain coming!!!

I was even a bit worried Friday evening when the forecast called for the thunderstorms to start back up at 1pm on Saturday.  Tony and I decided to get an early start to try and finish before then.  The forecast wasn't the last omen; I started the day off by waking up an hour late.  When I finally got in the car, Rise by Public Image Ltd came on.  The "I could be wrong, I could be right" lyrics kept repeating and knowing there is an easy recovery point at McGinnis Ferry which was only 3.5 miles into the trip had me second guessing our 8.5 mile route.  Then Queen & David Bowie came on next with Under Pressure.  

Yep, I was feeling the pressure to make a call and stick with it.  Knowing how long I'd been off the water, understanding all the car shuffling involved with making a canoe ride happening, and the deep desire to make some progress on the ATL50 - Canoeing thru the Chattahoochee River NRA goal I decided to suck it up and do the 8.5 – even if it meant we'd get drenched.  At the launch point at Settles Bridge I was able to try out my new portage cart and those little wheels make a HUGE difference when you have to carry your canoe more than 20 yards or so.  

I found this cart on Amazon for just under $50.  Looks like the price already went ahead – probably due to my review.    Most of the ones available are going for more than $100, so I took a chance and so far I'm thrilled with purchase.  I just wish I had bought it sooner.  Our launch point was Settles Bridge where Tony and I finished up ATL50 - Leg 1; Buford Dam to Settles Bridge many weeks ago.  As promised at the end of that posting, here are some photos of this very nice scenic canoe launch.  Here's a shot looking up river.

As expected, we ran into a little bit of interesting looking fog soon after we got started.

For a little while, we had a small group of kayaks a couple hundred yards in front of us.  Not long after we launched, I started hearing a pretty good rumble and then notice the kayaks take a little dip in the river ahead.  As we got closer, it was clear to see there was an almost perfectly smooth rock formation spanning the entire river which turned out to be a drop of about two feet.  Now, for you "experienced" canoeists, this is nothing to think about, but I'm still worried about screwing up on even the easy obstacles.  We approached the tiny fall feeling pretty good and just at the point I thought we were getting stuck I threw my right leg out thinking I'd jump out on that side to unjam us.  Of course, the boat kept on going and then we started overcompensating each other's movement; and oh yeah, my leg is still hanging out of the canoe!

Somehow we regained control before completely flipping the boat, but we did give a great show to the fella fly fishing nearby.  I wish we could have just done it again to get it right, but easier said than done once we past it.  To clear the record, this was MY fault – Tony did absolutely nothing wrong and stayed cool throughout our "little experience".    I tried to mark this tiny "falls" on my Kayaking & Canoeing Google Map., but simply could not locate it from those aerial photographs.  Probably a testament to truly how TINY they are.

We got down to McGinnis Ferry boat launch with little troubles and in good spirits.  Of course, it wasn't even the half-way point with 5 of 8.5 miles still left to cover before reaching the car.  We jumped back on the river and then a fisherman let us know he had just checked the forecast which has pushed the storms out.  That calmed us down and helped us pound through those next few miles.  The river from there down to Abbotts Bridge is pretty darn slow.  Again, I'm just grateful we did not get soaked.

We did travel under another old abandoned bridge; Rogers Bridge.  Rogers Bridge Park has a launch point there, too, but I have never visited it.

We finally got to the Abbotts Bridge launch just as about 20 "tubers" were finishing up their day, too.  I was grateful to see we could do this length trip fairly quickly (about 2 hours and 45 mins) and was especially grateful for not getting rained on!

My back was killing me though and when I got home I rigged up a way to lock down some old folding stadium seats to my canoe benches as seen below.

I can't wait to try them out.  Until next time, have fun and if you get out on the river; don't forget your life jacket!!
