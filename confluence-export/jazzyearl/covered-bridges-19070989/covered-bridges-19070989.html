Covered Bridges

2014-01-11 · Lester Martin

Page initially devoted to the covered bridges in Georgia, as identified on Wikipedia (and this list), but has expanded to include any, and all, covered bridges I’ve been fortunate enough to visit. As seen after the tables below, I'm maintaining my own Covered Bridges Google Map, too!

Focusing more on Georgia, here is a slightly longer list that I need to research; http://www.dalejtravis.com/cblist/cbga.htm (example: check out one spanning the Cartecay River).  There are also a few more detailed in Georgia Romantic Bridges.pdf and at https://www.exploregeorgia.org/things-to-do/blog/a-guide-to-georgias-covered-bridges that are not captured below.

Wooden bridges w/o covers had a 20 lifespan, but a covered wood bridge can survive 200 years. #coveredbridges https://t.co/O3jlmKcUSX

— Earls of Wisdom (@EarlsOfWisdom) February 28, 2021

Most #coveredbridges were painted red because it was the cheapest thing they could use to protect the wood on the outside. The red paint was a mixture of ingredients that were usually on-hand - skimmed milk, lime, and rust or oxidized iron.

— Earls of Wisdom (@EarlsOfWisdom) February 28, 2021
