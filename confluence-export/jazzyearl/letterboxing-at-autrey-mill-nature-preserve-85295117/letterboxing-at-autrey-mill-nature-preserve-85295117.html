Letterboxing at Autrey Mill Nature Preserve

2016-10-10 · Lester Martin

When the kids were younger, they had fun letterboxing with us.  Ok... maybe they just tolerated it.   

That said, they have not done it with us for years and Gretchen and I finally decided to get back out there by ourselves.  Sadly, since we We Closed on the NEW House!! we seem to have misplaced our letterboxing stamp and log book, so got a new book and stamp (we used to be the "4 Purple Martins").

While the www.letterboxing.org site I mentioned above is a good one, we have historically had better luck on www.atlasquest.com.  After some searching, we decided we would head over to Autrey Mill Nature Preserve & Heritage Center to check out the following boxes.

  • Sirius
  • Environmental Quest
  • Heritage Quest

After checking out the visitor center and the other "heritage" (aka OLD) buildings, we heading down the River Trail to find our first box; Sirius.  This was a textbook find that has a few simple clues/directions that would keep a family with small children (or adults wrapping up their 40's) engaged.  We were thrilled, we were able to stamp our new log book and ready to head on to the other two AtlasQuest entries by the same "planter"; especially since they were each stocked with three letterboxes each. 

That optimism quickly faded after we did not find any of the three Environmental Quest boxes.  The first and last clues were way too open-ended and the middle one got us to the right spot, but the box itself was missing.  No worries, we are out hiking and spending time together on a beautiful day.  We did cross a very interesting historical gold mine.  It is hard to see in the pictures below, but was a weird hole in the ground.

 

We decided to try our luck at the three boxes in the Heritage Quest set of clues.  Like before, the first and last clues did not help us any.  We were very glad to find the middle clue and get a second stamp in our book.  We headed for the car and found some more structures, including the teepee below, clearly used for group outings and maybe some summertime day camps.

While we only scored two of seven stamps today, it was clearly a success.  I love the outdoors we have here in Georgia – even in the middle of a busy Atlanta suburb where this preserve is located.  Gretchen and I highly recommend letterboxing to any families with small children who are looking for another outdoor activity to do with them.  It has been fun to us over the years.
